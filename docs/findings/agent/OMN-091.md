# OMN-091 — A partial withdrawal resets the remaining liquidity to fully available and unimpaired

**Kind:** sdk-defect  
**Severity:** high  
**Status:** fixed  
**Packages:** `@totemsdk/omnia-pool`
**Observed versions:** @totemsdk/omnia-pool@0.6.2
**Origin:** Omnia SDK audit — 25 November 2026 @ `9f882c8` (verified in source)
**Source:** `Omnia SDK audit — 25 November 2026, reporter finding OMN-089`

## Context

After a payout the remaining position is written with effectiveAmount = availableAmount = remaining and allocatedAmount = reservedAmount = 0n. That asserts the residual capital has no outstanding allocations, no reservations and no impairment — none of which the payout established.

## Impact

With residual obligations or impaired capital, the registry overstates immediately available liquidity, which is the number a withdrawal or Pool 001 capacity decision is made on.

## Reproduce

```bash
pnpm --filter @totemsdk/omnia-pool test
```

**Expected:** Outstanding allocations and reservations are preserved or reduced by the withdrawn amount, and effective-value adjustments survive.

**Actual:** A position with allocatedAmount 5000 and a partial withdrawal comes back with allocatedAmount 0.

**Evidence:**

- packages/omnia-pool/src/withdraw.ts:289-302 — the reset block

## Where to fix

- `packages/omnia-pool/src/withdraw.ts`

### Approach

Reconcile instead of resetting: subtract the withdrawn amount from allocated/reserved in the position's own terms, keep the impairment adjustment, and refuse the payout if the withdrawal would strand an allocation that cannot be reduced.

## Constraints (do not violate)

- Never raise available liquidity beyond what the pre-payout record justified.
- Preserve the effective-value ratio across a partial withdrawal.
- Refuse rather than guess when allocations exceed the remaining amount.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A partial withdrawal preserves outstanding allocations.
- [ ] An impaired position stays impaired after a partial withdrawal.
- [ ] The available amount never increases because of a withdrawal.
